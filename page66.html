After a long journey, Patricia finally arrived in New Zealand, the air crisp and smelling of the sea. Eager for a distraction and wanting to experience the local culture, she decided to head straight to Auckland to watch the legendary All Blacks rugby game. The city was buzzing with energy, a sea of fans clad in black streaming toward the stadium. She navigated through the bustling crowds, found her section, and settled into her seat, absorbing the electric atmosphere and the roar of the stadium as the pre-game excitement built up. She had been sitting there for a while, watching the players warm up on the pitch, when a man slipped into the empty seat right next to her. Before she could even turn her head, he spoke in a deeply familiar, raspy voice. "I'm glad you came to the game. I was worried that my adventure had killed you or something," he said, followed by a warm, booming laugh. Patricia gasped and turned around. It was her uncle! Shock washed over her, instantly replaced by sheer joy. She threw her arms around him, hugging him tightly as if to prove he was actually real and not a mirage.

As the stadium lights flared and the game kicked off, the action on the field became a backdrop to their long-awaited reunion. Patricia leaned in close to shout over the roaring crowd, eager to fill him in on everything. She told him about her frantic adventures around the world—how she had narrowly evaded the police in a high-stakes chase, and how, despite the chaos, she had witnessed breathtakingly beautiful sights in the most remote corners of the world. They talked about everything, pieces of the puzzle finally falling into place. Her uncle chuckled as he confessed to the clever tricks he used to evade her, explaining how he had managed to stay one step ahead during this wild goose chase across the globe. It felt like the chase was finally over, and a deep sense of relief settled over her. During a break in the match, her uncle stood up, stretching his legs. He smiled down at her, told her he was going to grab some food from the concourse, and asked if she wanted anything. Patricia shook her head, watching him disappear into the crowd. The game resumed, but as the minutes ticked by, he didn’t return. Halftime came and went, and his seat remained empty. Growing uneasy, Patricia looked down at the plastic stadium chair. Her heart dropped. Sitting right where her uncle had been was a small, folded piece of paper. With trembling fingers, she picked it up and opened it. Written in his familiar, hurried handwriting were just seven words: “Do you want to do it again?”
