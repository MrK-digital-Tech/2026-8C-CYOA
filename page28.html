Caught by the Police


Patricia leaned forward in the taxi, urging the driver to keep up as the car ahead weaved through the streets of Honolulu. Her eyes stayed locked on it—the same car she had seen the strangers climb into, the same people who had taken her uncle's notebook. Her heart pounded as they finally pulled over near a quiet beachfront car park. Trying to stay calm, Patricia paid quickly and slipped out of the taxi, keeping low as she watched the group step out and walk away, leaving the car unattended. This was her chance. She crept closer, glancing around before reaching for the door handle, her fingers trembling as she slowly opened it and spotted the familiar leatherbound notebook on the seat.
Just as she grabbed it, a firm voice called out behind her. “Excuse me—what do you think you’re doing?” Patricia froze. A police officer was walking towards her, clearly suspicious. She tried to explain, but the situation only seemed to get worse as the group returned, insisting the notebook was theirs. Within minutes, Patricia found herself being escorted away, her protests falling on deaf ears. By the next day, after a long and uncomfortable process, she was placed on a flight back to Australia. Sitting by the window, she stared out at the clouds, replaying everything that had gone wrong. The adventure wasn’t over—but for now, she had been forced out of it.
