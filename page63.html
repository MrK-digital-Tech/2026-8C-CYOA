Police don't like meddling children


Patricia faces disapproval from the police due to their disdain for what they perceive as meddling from children who lack proficiency in French. This language barrier exacerbates her situation, as it limits her ability to communicate effectively with the authorities. Consequently, her attempts to engage with them are met with resistance, leading to her being sent back home. The police's unwillingness to entertain her concerns reflects a broader issue of exclusion faced by those who do not meet certain linguistic or cultural expectations. Ultimately, Patricia's experience highlights the challenges of navigating authority without the necessary tools for effective dialogue.
